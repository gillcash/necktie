# Example: KPI data reliability for a tool and equipment rental store

Invoke Necktie with a decision, not a request to operate a workflow:

```text
/necktie Our tool and equipment rental store wants to rank branches and determine bonuses from utilization, dollar utilization, turnaround, maintenance cost, damage recovery, receivables, forward bookings, and repeat business. Assess whether those KPIs are trustworthy and whether tying compensation to them will improve the business.
```

Necktie should privately test the strongest case for aggressive metric-driven management, including its real benefits: comparability, faster intervention, accountability, and capital efficiency.

It should then test who controls each source event, which employees inherit the measurement burden, which costs disappear from the dashboard, how each metric can be gamed, and which failures can remain plausible across several KPIs.

The response should take a position. A likely recommendation is to avoid compensation based on unvalidated composite rankings, trace each KPI to authoritative source events, pair outcomes with quality and safety checks, and pilot the incentive with appeal and rollback mechanisms. That recommendation remains a hypothesis until supported by the store's actual systems, policies, and records.

In Lite and Full, Mammon must not appear as a speaker in the output. The user needs the decisive incentive analysis, not the private debate. In Mammon mode, return only Mammon's conclusion and omit Necktie's rebuttal.

## Turn the judgment into useful work

Full should offer to build a research prompt when the store's actual systems, source events, policies, or evidence are not yet known. Mammon should also offer a concrete artifact, although its research objective may prioritize control, leverage, and value capture.

If the user accepts, `necktie-research` should work backward from the intended operating package rather than emit a generic "research this topic" prompt. The prompt should encode:

- a specific one-branch Greater Moncton operating case;
- a reframed inquiry about process and control design instead of employee diligence;
- a reconciled cross-functional expert panel;
- a truth chain and explicit provenance classes;
- fifteen falsifiable hypotheses with a verdict schema;
- exact master-table columns and machine-readable deliverables;
- ten quantitative failure-propagation examples;
- startup and mature control frameworks;
- a 25-section report contract;
- a numbered source register, quality checks, expert objections, and strongest unasked question.

Those counts are properties of that case, not a universal template. The reusable principle is to fingerprint the target deliverable, preserve the required research intensity, and translate every material quality into an observable prompt requirement.

The research loop must scan the user-authorized context, capture the exact required schema, and structurally compare the prompt blueprint with any reference output before approval.
